# Sources and verification

Every external claim must come from a trusted source and pass verification. There is no fixed list of approved sources: the right sources depend on the document's subject, industry and geography, so choose them per claim using the criteria below.

## Plan the research

For each claim, ask who would produce authoritative data on it in this subject and place: the regulator or government body, the statistics office, the company itself (filings, official pages), the industry body, established analysts and research firms, established data publishers such as Statista, established news agencies such as Reuters, or peer-reviewed research. A healthcare claim in the UAE, a tax claim in the UK and a market figure in India each point to different authorities.

## The trust test

A source is trusted for a claim only if it passes all five:

| Test | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | It produced the data (official statistics, regulator, filing, study, survey, the company's own statement about itself), or it is an established publisher that verifies data and names its underlying source (e.g. Statista, Reuters) | It repeats a figure without saying where it came from |
| **Method** | The method, sample, period or dataset is stated or evident | No method, sample, period or definition |
| **Independence** | It gains nothing from the claim being true | A vendor's own "study" promoting what it sells; paid or sponsored content |
| **Currency** | Dated, and recent enough for the claim (prefer the last 3 years unless the claim is historical) | Undated, or superseded by newer data |
| **Fit** | Same geography, segment, period and definition as the claim | A US figure supporting a UK claim; "SMEs" supporting "startups" |

Usually fails, so use only as a lead to the original: vendor and agency blogs, "statistics roundup" pages, content-marketing reports, forums and social posts, AI-generated summaries, undated pages, Wikipedia (follow its citations instead). Syndicated market-report sellers often disagree widely with each other: cite one only when an independent source corroborates it, and label the figure as that publisher's forecast.

## Verification steps

1. **Open the page.** Snippets and summaries are leads, not evidence. The figure or statement must appear on the page you cite.
2. **Match exactly**: number, unit, currency, geography, period, and definition. If the source says something different, report what it actually says.
3. **Trace to origin.** If the page quotes someone else, follow it to where the data was produced, and cite both (e.g. "Statista, citing [statistics office], 2025").
4. **Corroborate High-risk claims** with a second independent trusted source. If credible sources disagree, report the range and which is more authoritative.
5. **Check freshness.** Look for a newer edition of the same data. A figure superseded by a newer release is Outdated even if it was once correct.
6. **For quotes**, find the original statement (transcript, recording, official release) and check the words and context.
7. **For people and companies**, check the official source (company website, regulatory filing, registry) and the date of the information.
8. **Record** publisher, title, date of publication, the period the data covers, URL, date accessed, and the exact sentence or table where the figure appears.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | A trusted source was opened and matches exactly |
| **Corroborated** | Verified by two independent trusted sources |
| **Outdated** | Was correct, but newer trusted data exists; give the current figure |
| **Mismatch** | The source says something different; state what it says |
| **Unsupported** | Traced only to untrusted sources (blogs, roundups, self-serving studies) |
| **Unverified** | No source found after a reasonable search |
| **Calculation error** | The document's own arithmetic is wrong; give the correct figure |
| **Inconsistent** | The document states it differently in different places |
| **Author to confirm** | The author's own data; internally consistent or not, only they can confirm |
| **Opinion / forecast** | Not a factual claim; labelled, not verified |
| **Broken link** | The link fails, or does not support the sentence |

## What to do with each result

- **Verified / Corroborated**: keep; add or correct the citation.
- **Outdated**: replace with the current figure and source.
- **Mismatch**: rewrite the sentence to say what the source says, or remove it.
- **Unsupported / Unverified**: find a trusted replacement, reword without the figure, or mark `[needs source]`. Never invent.
- **Calculation error / Inconsistent**: give the correct figure and every place it must change.
- **Author to confirm**: list for the author with any inconsistency noted.
